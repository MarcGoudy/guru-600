Patch Collection for the 2022 upgraded Prophet-600
Preliminary remarks

The idea of MIDI SysEx patches is, of course, that you can transfer patches created on one instrument to a computer and to another instrument
and in that way archive, manage and share sounds. However, on a VCO synthesizer like the Prophet-600 with its hardware related variations it can
be difficult to faithfully reproduce the sound of a patch from on a unit. While the VCO differences are handled by the hardware supported tuning
routine, there are other components which cannot be calibrated and which can have a significant impact on the sound, such as:


   • Filter resonance

   • Oscillator cross modulation

   • Pulse width


The patches documented below have been tested on a few different units and some care has gone into making them as transferable as possible.
Still, you should pay particular attention to those patches with a high level of oscillator FM / cross modulation (at least when sync is inactive).
Generally such patches are highly tuning sensitive. In addition, it may be necessary to adjust either oscillator A frequency or the poly-mod
oscillator B amount on your instrument in order to achieve a clean sounding patch. This is because, the absolute poly-mod level is not normalized
and slightly varies from instrument to instrument. Worst of all, there might be variations among the voices. If that is the case with your Prophet-
600, you may find it difficult to use strong ploy-mod in a clean and consistent way. Please refer to the user manual for more information on how
to use poly-mod properly.

The following 100 patches are grouped in 10 self-explanatory ”patch types”. For each patch some selected key information is provided. The idea
is that this is helpful to recognize how the sound of the patch is created and supports using those patches as a starting point for new patches.
In that sense the collection is not only intended to readily usable but is also to some degree educational about the features of the Prophet-600.
The additional information is as follows:


   • Envelope Routing: This shows if a non-standard routing is used. The comment may indicate why this waa chosen.

   • Oscillator FM: This gives an indication of the strength of poly-mod oscillator modulation, i.e. when target oscillator A is active the number
     of stars indicate the amount of oscillator B source.

      • Filter FM: this gives an indication of the strength of poly-mod filter modulation, i.e. when target filter is active the number of stars indicate
        the amount of oscillator B source.

      • Sync: Indicates whether oscillator sync is active.

      • Harmonic filter: This indicates if full tracking is active with a filter resonance close to self-resonance. Under these conditions the filter serves
        as chromatically tuned third oscillator.

      • Unison: This indicates if the patch uses unison.

      • Velocity: The default setting is that patch velocity is medium for amplitude and zero for filter cut-off. The velocity information marks patches
        for which more depth in expression is supported when played via external MIDI.

      • Comments: Any specific useful information about the patch.


Note to developers: the patches contain patch names according to the MIDI specification. The Prophet-600 cannot (yet) display patch names, but
they could be shown in MIDI patch editors.
